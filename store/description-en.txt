Control your own Music Assistant server from SailfishOS. Not affiliated with the Music Assistant project — the name belongs to its authors.

FEATURES

- Every player of your setup at a glance, with what is playing, a progress bar and play/pause right in the row. Unavailable players stay visible, just dimmed.
- A page per player with artwork, title, artist, album, a seekable progress slider, next and previous, volume and mute.
- Library by artists, albums, tracks, playlists and radio, with a count per section, loaded page by page and searchable per list. Album and artist pages with artwork, playlists with their tracks.
- Search across all media types on one page, grouped by type, covering the library and the connected providers.
- Play from any list: play now, queue next, or append. Which player it lands on is a choice you make once.
- Mark and unmark favourites, shown as a star in the list; any list can be switched to favourites only.
- View and edit the queue: tap to jump to a track, move entries up, down or to the end, remove them one by one. Shuffle, crossfade and repeat. Hand the whole queue to another player, save it as a playlist, or clear it.
- Group speakers so several play the same thing in sync, with one volume for the group. Only speakers the server can actually synchronise are offered.
- Cover view with the running track, artwork and play/pause — usable without opening the app.
- Lock screen and media keys drive the remote player over MPRIS. Title, artist, album, artwork and position show up there; the app itself outputs no audio.
- Optional notification on track change, off by default.
- Everything updates through server events rather than polling: changes made elsewhere show up immediately.
- Portrait orientation can be locked.

REQUIREMENTS

- A Music Assistant server reachable on your local network (default port 8095). If it runs as a Home Assistant app, that is the address of the HA machine, not the ingress address.
- An access token, created in the Music Assistant interface under Settings → Profile.

Address and token are stored encrypted in Sailfish Secrets and tied to the device lock.

A NOTE ON THE AUDIO PERMISSION

The app asks for the Audio permission although it neither records nor plays anything. On SailfishOS that is the permission which allows registering an MPRIS service — that is, the controls on the lock screen. There is no finer-grained way to get there.
